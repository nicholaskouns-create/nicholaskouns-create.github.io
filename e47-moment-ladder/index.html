The Moment Ladder

The E47 moment selector cuts two whole Casimir sectors on the cube, 125 → 47. Every tensor order where it can even try sits on a Pell ladder, and on the next three rungs, n = 25, 243 and 2401, its two roots never meet.

Above them every rung allows at most finitely many spins, and through the ninth rung there are none below 101000. That answers the question the selector theorem left open on 15 October: proved through n = 2401, finite on every rung, and searched far beyond.

TheoremE0

The Moment Ladder theorem

On the carrier H = Vj⊗n with total Casimir C, write c = j(j+1) and let μ and σ² be the mean and variance of spec C under the normalized trace. The moment selector is Kmom = (C − (μ−σ)I)(C − (μ+σ)I), and the corpus lemma gives μ = nc, σ² = (2n(n−1)/3) c². A root lands when it equals a Casimir value J(J+1) of a sector present in H.

I

The ladder E0 · P1

σ is rational exactly when n = (x+1)/2 with x² − 6y² = 1. The rungs are n = 1, 3, 25, 243, 2401, 23763, … and there σ = y c. Off the ladder no root is rational, so Kmom is invertible on every carrier.

The chain E0 · P2

III

Where both roots land E0 · P3–P5E1

Both roots land, giving a kernel of two whole sectors, on rung 1 (n = 3) exactly when 2j+1 = Fk with k odd. On rungs 2, 3 and 4 (n = 25, 243, 2401) it never happens for j > 0. On every rung k ≥ 2 it happens for at most finitely many spins, and on rungs 5 to 9 for none with 2j+1 < 101000.

IV

The count E0 · P6

On the Fibonacci–Lucas family the upper sector has multiplicity Fk−2, so the kernel has dimension Fk² + Fk−2Lk. On the cube that is 47 = 5² + 2·11, and along the family Fk · Ω(k) → 3/φ.

ProofsE0

Six short proofs

P1

Rational σ lives on a Pell ladder

σ/c = √(2n(n−1)/3). If this is rational, then 6n(n−1) = 9(σ/c)² is an integer that is a rational square, hence an integer square r². Since 3 divides r², write r = 3y, so 2n(n−1) = 3y². Put x = 2n − 1; then x² − 1 = 4n(n−1) = 6y².

x² − 6y² = 1,   x + y√6 = (5 + 2√6)k,   σ = y c

Off the ladder μ ± σ is irrational while every Casimir value J(J+1) is rational, so no root lands.∎

P2

Each upper root is the next lower root

Consecutive solutions satisfy (xk, yk) = (5xk−1 + 12yk−1, 2xk−1 + 5yk−1). Then

nk − yk = (xk−1 + 2yk−1 + 1)/2 = nk−1 + yk−1

so the lower root μ − σ on rung k is the upper root μ + σ of rung k − 1. Since (√2 + √3)² = 5 + 2√6, the coefficients are sk = (1 + uk)/2 with u = 1, 9, 89, 881, 8721, …∎

P3

n = 25: the 9 : 1 obstruction

The roots are 5c and 45c, so a double landing needs J₂(J₂+1) = 9 J₁(J₁+1). With B = 2J+1 this is

9B₁² − B₂² = 8,   (3B₁ − B₂)(3B₁ + B₂) = 8

Both factors are positive with equal parity, and the larger is at least 4. The only split is 2 · 4, which gives B₁ = B₂ = 1, so J₁ = J₂ = 0 and c = 0.∎

P4

n = 243 and 2401: the 441 wall

The coefficient 441 = 21² is the upper root of rung 3 and the lower root of rung 4. With a = 2j+1, the equation J(J+1) = 441 j(j+1) reads

(21a)² − B² = 440,   (21a − B)(21a + B) = 440

For a ≥ 2 the factor 21a + B is at least 43. The even splits of 440 above that, 2·220, 4·110 and 10·44, give 42a = 222, 114 and 54, none divisible by 42. So this root never lands for j > 0, and neither rung can land twice.∎

P5

Genus zero on the cube, genus one above it

A double landing on rung k is an integer point on two quadrics, with s = sk−1 and s′ = sk:

b² − s a² = 1 − s,   d² − s′a² = 1 − s′

Their pencil degenerates at 0, ∞, −s′/s and −(s′−1)/(s−1). These are distinct whenever s, s′ ∉ {0, 1} and s ≠ s′, which holds on every rung k ≥ 2. The curve is then smooth of genus one, and Siegel’s theorem leaves finitely many integer points. On the cube s = 1: the first quadric splits into b = ±a (the lower root always lands, at J = j), and what remains is the conic b² − 5a² = −4 of genus zero, whose integer points are the Fibonacci–Lucas pairs.∎

The obstruction above the cube is integrality. The curves carry rational points of infinite order (rank 1 on rungs 2 and 3, rank 2 on rungs 4 and 5, by PARI 2-descent, E1), and on rung 2 the rational spin j = 98/1501 lands both roots, at 2J+1 = 2321/1501 and 5519/1501.

P6

The Fibonacci count

In Vj⊗3 = Vj ⊗ (V0 ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ V2j), a spin J with j ≤ J ≤ 3j occurs once for each L with J − j ≤ L ≤ 2j, so 3j − J + 1 times, and spin j occurs 2j+1 times. With 2j+1 = Fk, 2J+1 = Lk and Lk = Fk + 2Fk−1:

3j − J + 1 = (3Fk − Lk)/2 = Fk−2,   dim ker = Fk² + Fk−2Lk

For odd k, Fk−2Lk = F2k−2 + 1, so FkΩ = 1 + (F2k−2 + 1)/Fk² → 1 + √5/φ² = 3/φ.∎
